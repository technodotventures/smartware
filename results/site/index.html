What ORP specifies

Rank only what the asker may read

Access is decided before scoring, statistics come from readable memories only, and recall must equal recall over the store with everything unreadable removed.

Three clocks

Observed, happened and valid time on every record; every query carries an as-of time, so answers never drift with the wall clock.

Supersession you can audit

Newer claims replace older ones by validity time, only with write permission and never hiding a fact from someone who cannot see its replacement.

Context, not lists

Recall returns a token-budgeted, cited, explained context pack with coverage and degradation flags.

Derived never outranks its sources

Summaries and profiles inherit the narrowest audience and the sensitivity labels of what they were built from.

Testable

70 numbered requirements, each mapped to a conformance test, plus this benchmark with its raw per-question results.

Final test: blind question answering, two independent readers pre-registered

232 official LongMemEval-S test questions never used in an earlier answer test. Every one of the 15 systems, BM25 and the gold-evidence ceiling, 1,000 tokens of context each. One reader and judge was Claude Sonnet, the other DeepSeek V3; neither knew which system produced a context. ORP v0.1.3 writes a date on every line, a change chosen on separate dev questions with both readers.

Claude: better than 15 of 16, level with 1, worse than 0DeepSeek: better than 10 of 16, level with 6, worse than 0

With DeepSeek the gold evidence alone scores only 61.6%, so all the leading systems are squeezed into the few points below that ceiling; ORP v0.1.3 ties for the top there. The v0.1.3 change itself lifted DeepSeek accuracy from 51.7% to 58.6% without costing anything with Claude.

Blind question answering on official LongMemEval-S pre-registered

The end-to-end test: 150 test questions, every one of the 15 systems plus BM25 and ORP, 1,000 tokens of context each. Claude Sonnet readers answered without knowing which system produced the context; separate judges graded with LongMemEval's official rules, also blind. A second judge pass agreed on 99.8% of 540 items.

ORP 80.7% against 72.7% for the best of the 15 (Graphiti (Zep)); significantly ahead of 16 of 16 systems after Holm correction. Gold evidence alone scores 95.3%.

A non-Claude reader does not confirm the lead. The same blind items read and judged by DeepSeek V3: ORP 46.0%, best of the 15 56.0% (mem0; ORP minus -10.0, 95% CI [-18.0, -2.0], Holm p 0.13). Gold evidence alone scores only 61.3% with this reader: it answers "I don't know" on many questions that need several facts combined, and did so most often on ORP's contexts. ORP's end-to-end advantage depends on a reader that reasons across its contexts.

Read these numbers with care

  • The 15 are re-implementations of each project's recall path without an LLM, written from its source code, not the products. Projects that rely on LLM extraction or reranking are represented by deterministic stand-ins.
  • Every system uses the same modest encoder (PPMI-SVD word vectors), trained without labels on the benchmark text. A strong neural embedder could change the order.
  • ORP had a larger tuning budget than any single baseline. The hand-tuned, model-free ORP variant is shown alongside for a like-for-like comparison of architecture.
  • LME-R is a reconstruction of LongMemEval; the LongMemEval-S and -M sections use the official haystacks. Recall numbers here are not comparable to published LoCoMo or LongMemEval answer accuracies.
  • Test results from the pre-audit version had been seen before the current engine was built, so LoCoMo and LME-R are not pristine held-out sets for it. HotpotQA-50 is.
Specification: ORP 0.1 draft. Methodology, two independent audits and raw per-question results ship with the repository.
